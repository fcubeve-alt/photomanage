## 1. The question, and what does not count

Tier 0 v1.1 §4 sets the bar:

> 至少用以下一种方式获取**真实付费信号**，而不是问卷式假设… 在「持续自动管理」或更高层级，能拿到真实意向付费信号（不只是问卷好感度）。

**Rejected as evidence:** surveys, interest scores, "would you pay?" questions, friendly opinions, email signups with no price shown. All of these are free to give and mean nothing (PF-03).

**Accepted:** a person who saw a real price and took a deliberate action to buy at it.

**Rung constraint, from C1:** the Cleaner rung is commoditised — ten cleaners free to install, the top one with 701k ratings, one competitor giving cleaning away outright. Willingness to pay *there* proves nothing. The signal must come from **Continuous Automatic Management or above**.

## 2. Ethics — decided before anything else, because it constrains the design

This is a fake-door test: a page selling something that does not exist yet. Done carelessly that is a lie told to strangers for data.

**Rules, non-negotiable:**

1. **No money is ever taken.** The Tier 0 doc itself says to reach *"真实支付环节前一步"* — the step immediately before charging. We stop there.
2. **Disclosure at the moment of truth.** The instant someone commits to buy, they see the truth before anything else:
   > **We're not charging you.**
   > Personal Visual Memory isn't out yet — you've just helped us prove people want it.
   > Leave your email and you'll get first access at this price, locked in.
3. **No dark patterns.** No fake countdowns, no fake scarcity, no fake review counts, no invented company. The page describes a product we intend to build, in the future tense where appropriate.
4. **No purchase can be completed by accident.** The disclosure is a full-screen interstitial, not a footnote.
5. **Honest ad copy.** Ads do not claim the app is available today.

This costs some conversion. It buys a signal we can actually believe, and it keeps us on the right side of the trust the whole product depends on (§26).

**A deposit variant is the stronger signal and is deliberately NOT the default.** Taking even $3 creates a real obligation, refund handling, and possible tax/consumer-law exposure. Hold it in reserve for the case in §7.

## 3. What is being manipulated — rung, not price

The Tier 0 kill question is *which rung of the Value Ladder carries willingness to pay*, not *what is the optimal price*. So **positioning is the manipulation and price is held constant.**

Three landing pages, identical layout, identical price, identical CTA mechanics. Only the value proposition differs.

| Arm | Rung | Thesis it tests |
|---|---|---|
| **L1 · Organised Library** | Visual Library | The DEC-012 differentiator: open it and your library is already catalogued; this becomes where your photos live (§12, §22) |
| **L2 · Continuous Manager** | Continuous Automatic Management | The rung Tier 0 §4 names: you stop managing photos, permanently (§13) |
| **L3 · Cleaner** | Cleaner | **Control.** The commodity pitch. Needed to interpret L1/L2 — without it, a low number on L1 is uninterpretable |

L3 is not a candidate positioning — Constitution §24 Gate 4 forbids shipping as a Cleaner. It is a **measuring stick**. If the commodity pitch beats both of ours, that is a severe finding and we need to know it here, not after building.

**Price: $39 one-off, "lifetime, no subscription".**
Justification from C1: every credible non-predatory competitor lands at $29.99–$49.99 one-off (Slidebox $29.99/$49.99, Clever Cleaner $39.99, CleanMy®Phone $34.99, Lucent Pro $29.99). Weekly subscription is excluded on trust grounds — the category monetises at $4–12/week ($207–623/yr) with documented tracking-after-payment and price doubling, and our product's entire premise is trust (§26).

> **Open risk, to be modelled in Tier 2-F, not resolved here:** a one-off price cannot fund indefinite maintenance. We are testing whether people will pay at this rung, using the price the market has taught them is fair. Sustainability is a separate question and must not be silently assumed away.

## 5. Funnel and instrumentation

| # | Event | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Ad impression | reach |
| E2 | Ad click → LP view | interest in the pitch |
| E3 | Scroll ≥ 50% | pitch held attention |
| E4 | **Price block viewed** | saw a real number |
| E5 | **Clicked "Get it — $39"** | ← **PRIMARY. The deliberate act after seeing a price.** |
| E6 | Disclosure interstitial shown | — |
| E7 | Email captured at $39 | commitment survives the truth |
| E8 | "No thanks" after disclosure | wanted the product, not the waitlist |

**Primary metric: E5 / E2 — click-to-buy rate per landing-page visitor.**
Secondary: E7/E5 (does intent survive disclosure), E4→E5 (does the price kill it), E3 (did the pitch land at all).

**Attribution:** each arm on its own URL (`/a`, `/b`, `/c`), traffic split evenly, arm recorded server-side on every event.

### Privacy — this page must not contradict the product (§26)
- **No third-party ad pixel on the landing page.** First-party event logging only, server-side.
- Conversion reported back to ad platforms via **server-side conversion API** with a hashed click id — no client-side tracker following visitors around.
- Cookie banner: essential only. No consent theatre.
- Email addresses: stored in one place, used only for launch notification, deletable on request, never sold.
- Analytics: self-hosted or a cookieless provider. **No Google Analytics.**

> Running a privacy-first product's own ad test on surveillance infrastructure would be the first broken promise, made before we ship anything. Not worth the convenience.

## 6. PASS criteria — pre-registered before any traffic (AB-7 discipline)

Sample: **≥ 400 landing-page visitors per arm** (1,200 total). Below ~400/arm the confidence interval on a 2% rate is too wide to act on.

| ID | Criterion | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **C-P1** | **PRIMARY** — E5/E2 on the better of L1/L2 | **≥ 2.5%** |
| **C-P2** | Better of L1/L2 beats L3 (Cleaner control) on E5/E2 | **strictly greater**, and by ≥ 1pp |
| **C-P3** | Intent survives disclosure: E7/E5 | **≥ 50%** |
| **C-P4** | The price is not the blocker: E5/E4 | **≥ 15%** |

**PASS** = C-P1 **and** C-P2. There is a real, priced signal at a rung above Cleaner.

**GO WITH CONSTRAINTS** = C-P1 passes, C-P2 fails → people will pay, but the commodity pitch converts as well or better. Positioning problem, not a demand problem. Re-test copy before Tier 2 spends anything on unit economics.

**FAIL** = C-P1 fails → **COMMERCIAL MODEL PIVOT, not NO-GO.** Per Tier 0 v1.1 and DEC-002, a C failure does not kill the product. It triggers a re-test of price, free/Pro boundary, one-off vs subscription, and whether any model covers long-term maintenance. Technical and product work continues.

> **On the 2.5% threshold — stated plainly:** this is a judgement call, not a measured norm. Cold-traffic conversion for an unknown paid app varies enormously by channel and audience. It is set here, in advance, so it cannot drift to meet the data (AB-7). If the result lands close to it, report the number and the confidence interval and let the Owner decide — do not quietly reinterpret the threshold.

## 7. If the signal is ambiguous — the deposit escalation

If C-P1 lands between roughly 1.5% and 2.5%, click-to-buy is not decisive. **Then, and only then**, escalate to the stronger instrument:

- A **$5 refundable deposit** securing the $39 price, on a fresh cohort.
- Money genuinely taken, genuinely refundable on request, with a stated expected launch window and an unconditional refund if we do not ship.
- This is a real financial and legal commitment → **Owner decision, HG-2b**, not something to slide into.

A deposit converts perhaps a tenth as often as a free click, but it is the difference between *intent* and *behaviour*, and Tier 0 §4 explicitly lists it as an accepted instrument.

## 8. Traffic plan and cost

| Channel | Fit | Est. CPC | Cost for 1,200 visits |

| **Reddit ads** (r/iphone, r/ios, r/DataHoarder, r/declutter) | Best — targeted, tolerant of pre-launch, cheap | $0.30–0.80 | **~$400–950** |

**Recommendation: Reddit ads, ~$500–700 for the full three-arm test.** Cheapest route to a statistically usable sample, and the audience is right.

**Plus:** domain ~$12/yr, hosting free tier (static page + one serverless endpoint for event logging and email capture).

**➡️ Total to a defensible T0-C2 result: roughly $520–720.** This is a materially larger ask than the €2.64 Mac block and is a separate Owner decision (**HG-2b**). Nothing is spent without explicit approval.

## 9. Sequencing — T0-D should run first

**T0-D tells us whether the rung is even wanted; T0-C2 tells us whether it is paid for.** Running C2 first risks spending ~$600 advertising a value proposition that T0-D would have shown users do not want.

Recommended order: **T0-D → adjust L1/L2 copy using the actual language participants used → T0-C2.**

Concretely, T0-D outputs that should rewrite this copy before it goes live:
- The verbatim words participants use for "it's already organised" → the L1 hero
- Their stated conditions for trusting automation (§7 trust probe) → the L2 proof points
- The measured Human Review Burden crossover → replaces the invented "23 things" in the L2 subhead with a number that actually reads as *handled* rather than *homework*

If T0-D is blocked for a long time on participant recruitment, C2 can run first — but the copy is then unvalidated guesswork and the report must say so.
